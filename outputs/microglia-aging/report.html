How the score works

Each in-scope study gets four components between 0 and 1. The score is their weighted sum times 100. Weights live in the run configuration, so they can be debated and changed without touching code.

ComponentWeight
Metadata completeness30%
Design fit (young and old groups)25%
Target material (the cells the question asks about)25%
Data type fit10%
Traceability (paper and raw data)10%

Completeness is the mean share of samples with age, sex, tissue or cell type, and strain or genotype. Design fit is 1 when there are at least 3 young and 3 old baseline samples, 0.5 when both groups exist but are smaller, and 0 otherwise. Baseline means untreated and wild type; unknown counts as baseline. For mouse, young is 1.5 to 6 months and old is 18 months or more. Data type fit: bulk RNA-seq 1.0, single-cell RNA-seq 0.8, microarray 0.6. Traceability adds 0.5 for a linked publication and 0.5 for referenced raw data.

Limits and related work

  • Only metadata is assessed. Expression values, batch effects and sequencing quality are not checked.
  • Harmonization uses rules and a small controlled vocabulary. It favours leaving a value empty over guessing, so coverage is lower than recall-oriented tools and every value can be traced to its rule.
  • Ontology identifiers were entered by hand and should be confirmed against the EBI Ontology Lookup Service before downstream use.
  • GEO search ranks by its own relevance. Studies that describe the topic with other words are not found.
  • MetaSRA and refine.bio already normalize public sample metadata at scale. Dataset Scout does not replace them: it answers one question at a time, with transparent, configurable scoring and a manual check of its own accuracy.
